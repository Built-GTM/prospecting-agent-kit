# Signals: what to search for, and what it means

A signal is only useful with a freshness window and a problem it points at. Both are required, or the agent will reach out about something that stopped mattering months ago.

## Tier 1: catalysts
Something happened, and it created work for someone.
| Signal | Fresh for | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| [new location, acquisition, funding, new leader in the owning role] | [30 to 90 days] | [news, their site, LinkedIn] |

## Tier 2: symptoms
Nothing happened, but the strain shows.
| Signal | Fresh for | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| [hiring for roles that imply the pain, reviews complaining about it] | [60 to 120 days] | [careers page, G2, Google reviews] |

## Tier 3: intent and absence
The quietest and often the most honest. An absence signal has no event date: label it "absence, checked [today's date]".
| Signal | Fresh for | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| [no online booking, no customer portal, a competitor's widget on the site] | [absence, check on the day] | [their own website] |

## Search recipes
Write the literal query. The agent runs what you write here, so vagueness costs you.
| To find | Run |
|---|---|
| [a new location] | `"[company]" "now open" OR "new location" 2026` |
| [whether they run a competitor] | `site:[competitor-domain] "[company]"` |

## Known vendor domains
Domains that mean a prospect already runs a competitor. Not a stop, but a strong Why now.
| Domain | Vendor |
|---|---|
| `[booking.competitor.com]` | [competitor] |

## One level deeper
For each Tier 1 signal, the question behind the headline. Not "they opened a location" but "who is answering the phones for it". This is the line that makes a brief sound like a person wrote it.
