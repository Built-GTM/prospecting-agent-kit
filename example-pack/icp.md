## The state they're in when they buy
Growing faster than the office can keep up. More calls than the CSRs can answer, technicians whose numbers nobody can see, invoices that sit, or a deal that just left them running two companies on two systems. (inferred, supported by the four value claims in `company.md`)

## Disqualifiers
A disqualifier never stops the brief. It becomes a caution at the top, with its source.
| Disqualifier | Threshold or example |
|---|---|
| Already running ServiceTitan | Any domain in the customer check table in `company.md`. This one does stop the brief. |
| Solo operator or one truck | No office staff, owner answers the phone, under about 5 technicians. Housecall Pro and Jobber territory. |
| New construction only | Builds only, no service or replacement work, so no dispatch or service agreements |
| Franchisor owned software decision | A national franchise brand where corporate picks the system. Check who decides before reaching out. |
| Not a trade at all | Anything outside the trades in `company.md` |
| Already on a direct competitor | Not a disqualifier. It is a competitor signal for Why now, and it belongs in the brief. |

## Low confidence, check rather than conclude
- The 10 to 250 technician sweet spot is inferred from per technician pricing, not stated. Treat a smaller or larger company as a question, not a rejection.
- LinkedIn headcount undercounts trades businesses badly, because technicians rarely keep profiles. Use trucks, locations, review volume, or job posts for size instead.
